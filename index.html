What is Vector Pad

Vector Pad is a front-end for open protocols on Solana. It gives you one interface for four things: launching a token, swapping, staking SOL, and bridging between Solana and Ethereum. Underneath, it talks to protocols that already live on-chain — Raydium for pools, Jupiter for swap routing, Solana's native stake program, and Wormhole for bridging.

It is not a custodian. Vector Pad holds no funds, runs no accounts, and has no admin keys over your assets. There is nothing to deposit and nothing to withdraw from Vector Pad itself, because your assets never leave your wallet or the protocol you are using.

How every action works

Every transaction follows the same pattern:

  1. Build — the app prepares the transaction in your browser.
  2. Simulate — it is simulated on-chain first. If simulation fails, nothing is sent and you see why.
  3. Summary — you see a one-line summary: network, wallet, exact amounts.
  4. Sign — your wallet asks you to approve. If you didn't sign it, it didn't happen.

The pages

  • — the launches board and token pages.
  • — trade any Solana token through Jupiter routing.
  • — native SOL staking with a validator you choose.
  • — move assets between Solana and Ethereum via Wormhole.
  • — mint a token and open its first pool in two transactions.
  • — your balances, holdings and stake, read live from the chain.

Getting started

1. Connect a wallet

Click Connect wallet in the header and choose Phantom or Solflare. Your wallet connects read-first: the app can show your address and balances, but it can never move anything without a signature from you. Browsing — the board, token pages, quotes — never needs a wallet at all.

2. Mainnet by default — devnet for practice

Vector Pad runs on Solana mainnet by default: real funds, real pools. The Mainnet / Devnet switch in the header flips the whole app to Solana devnet, where SOL is free from the faucet and nothing has real value — use it to rehearse a complete token launch (mint + Raydium pool) before spending real SOL. Your wallet keeps the same address on both networks; balances and pools are separate per network, and on devnet you can pair with SOL or devnet USDC only. Every transaction is simulated before you sign, on both networks.

3. That's it — no account

There is no sign-up, no email, no password. Your wallet address is your identity here, and disconnecting from the wallet menu in the header ends the session. Nothing about you is stored by this app.

Before your first transaction

  • Keep a little SOL in your wallet beyond what you plan to spend — every transaction pays a small network fee (0.000005 SOL per signature), and creating things also pays refundable rent deposits. See .
  • Read the summary line before signing, and check the confirmation screen inside your wallet — that screen is the final word.
  • New to Solana? Start with a small amount first while you learn the flow.

Explore

Explore is the launches board: tokens launched through Vector Pad, alongside live Solana market data. Prices, volume and trades come from live sources (DEXScreener and GeckoTerminal) — when a source can't be reached, the board says so instead of showing stale numbers.

Reading a token page

  • Price and chart — the current price, its change over the selected window, and a price chart with timeframe switches.
  • Key stats — market cap, liquidity, volume and holder-side figures where the data source provides them. Thin liquidity means small trades move the price a lot.
  • Risk checks — a plain-language list of what to look at before trading: whether supply can still be minted, how concentrated holdings are, and how deep the pool is.
  • Technical details — the mint (contract) address, decimals, total supply and authority status, each linking out to a Solana explorer.

Verify the contract address

Tickers are not unique — anyone can launch a token with the same name and symbol. The mint address is the only real identity. Before you trade or send anything:

  1. Copy the mint address from the token page in this app — don't retype it from a screenshot or a post.
  2. Paste it into a Solana explorer such as Solscan and confirm the token name, supply and pool match what you saw here.
  3. Compare the whole address, not just the first and last few characters — impostor addresses are crafted to match at both ends.

Swap

Swap asks Jupiter for a live quote across Solana mainnet liquidity — the route may pass through several pools to get you the best available output.

The flow

  1. Pick the pair and enter an amount. The quote updates live: expected output, price impact, minimum received and the route.
  2. The transaction is simulated on-chain. If it fails simulation, nothing is sent.
  3. You see a one-line summary, then sign in your wallet. The final confirmation always happens there, not here.

Slippage

Slippage is the most the executed price may move against you compared with the quote. If the price moves beyond your setting before the transaction lands, the swap fails instead of filling at a bad price. A tight setting protects your price but fails more often in fast markets; a loose setting fills more reliably but accepts worse prices. The minimum-received figure in the quote already accounts for your slippage setting.

What it costs

Trades execute in pools that charge the standard Raydium CPMM fee of 0.25% of each trade, handled by the Raydium program and shared under its rules between liquidity providers and the protocol. Vector Pad adds no platform fee on swaps — the Jupiter quote is the rate the transaction targets, subject to your slippage. Plus Solana's network fee of 0.000005 SOL per signature. Full breakdown in .

Stake

Stake uses Solana native staking — no wrapped tokens, no third-party staking pool. Your SOL moves into a stake account that you own and is delegated to a validator you choose. Delegation gives the validator voting weight; it never gives them spending power over your SOL.

How staking works

  • Stake — enter an amount (minimum 0.01 SOL) and pick a validator from the verified list, or paste any vote account address — it is checked on-chain before use. Signing creates the stake account and delegates it in your wallet.
  • Activation — new stake warms up over the next epoch boundary (an epoch is roughly two days) and then starts earning.
  • Rewards — paid by the protocol each epoch into your stake account, minus the validator's commission, which is shown next to their name. Reward rates vary by validator and epoch and are never guaranteed.
  • Deactivate — starts a cooldown of roughly 1–2 epochs. During cooldown the stake earns nothing and cannot be withdrawn early.
  • Withdraw — once the account is fully inactive, withdraw the full balance back to your wallet.

Costs

There is no Vector Pad fee. A stake account needs a rent-exempt reserve of ≈0.00228 SOL, held inside the account — it is refundable: withdraw in full and the account closes, returning the reserve to your wallet. Your stake accounts and their live status are listed on the Stake page and in .

Bridge

The Bridge page embeds Wormhole Connect, Wormhole's official widget. Transfers are executed by the Wormhole protocol and its guardian network — Vector Pad never touches the assets in transit.

Directions

  • Deposit — Solana → Ethereum.
  • Withdraw — Ethereum → Solana.

Supported assets include SOL and ETH and the other tokens the widget quotes a route for. Connect the source and destination wallets inside the widget — a Solana wallet for the Solana side, an Ethereum wallet for the Ethereum side.

Fees and timing

There is no fixed bridge price list: the widget quotes the bridge fee, any relayer cost and the expected arrival time live, for the exact route you picked, before you confirm. Vector Pad adds nothing on top. Routes with an automatic relayer deliver straight to your destination wallet; manual routes ask you to claim on the destination chain inside the widget — if you closed the tab, Resume Transaction in the widget picks the transfer back up.

Before you bridge

A Solana address and an Ethereum address are different formats, and a bridge transfer cannot be reversed or rerouted. Paste the destination address from the wallet that should receive the funds, and check it in full before you sign. Fees come out of the transfer, so very small amounts can arrive as almost nothing — read the live quote.

Create token

The Create wizard takes you through six steps:

  1. Identity — name, ticker, image and description.
  2. Pair — what the pool trades against: SOL, USDC, or a tokenized stock (xStock).
  3. Fee — the trading-fee setting you want associated with the launch.
  4. Coin type — the launch profile for the token.
  5. Payouts — wallet addresses and shares for creator payouts.
  6. Review & Execute — check the summary card, then deploy.

Two launch modes

Bonding curve (pump.fun-style) — one create_curve call to the Vector Pad bonding-curve program (3P1BD4cwPHAozg7unLo28kBj7Bm3W9mTtVrK3b6s21S7). The program creates the mint, holds the full 1B supply in the curve vault (793.1M for sale, 206.9M reserved for the graduation pool), and revokes the mint authority. Virtual reserves start at 30 SOL / 1.073B tokens; trades pay a 1% fee (0.70% protocol to FQQpuHZWMowwVNWoYNjovdMA9FqLPqSWmddaAdRFHLCb, 0.30% creator, paid out by collect_fees). Your wallet gets zero tokens at create — you buy on the curve like everyone else. At ~85 SOL of net buys the curve completes; anyone can then call graduate (permissionless, ~0.02 SOL buffer refunded to the caller), which opens the Raydium CPMM pool from the curve's collected SOL and burns the LP. After graduation the token page switches to Jupiter / Raydium quoting and bonding buy/sell switches off. Create cost: ~0.01 SOL of rent only. The program is built and unit-tested (8/8) and deployed on mainnet (21 Nov 2026) — bonding mode is live there. On a network where it is not deployed (e.g. devnet), the app says so plainly (bonding curve launches when the program is deployed) and sends nothing in this mode.

Direct Raydium — the original two-transaction flow below, unchanged.

What deploys on-chain (Direct Raydium)

Deployment is two wallet transactions — mint first, pool second:

  • Mint — a Token-2022 token with a fixed supply of 1,000,000,000 (6 decimals). The mint and freeze authority are revoked in the same transaction, so no more can ever be issued and balances can't be frozen. Cost: ≈0.0035 SOL in rent-exempt deposits, paid to the network.
  • Pool — a Raydium CPMM pool seeded with the liquidity you provide. Cost: ≈0.19 SOL for pool account rent and Raydium's creation cost, on top of the liquidity itself. The LP tokens land in your wallet.

From there the pool trades on Raydium and routes through Jupiter like any other Solana pool, and the token shows up in Explore, Swap and Portfolio.

Honest note — what is display-only today

The fee percentage, coin type and payout splits picked in steps 3–5 are currently saved as display metadata only. Pools open with Raydium's standard 0.25% fee configuration, creator fee allocation is not yet wired on-chain, and LP tokens are not auto-locked — they sit in your wallet, under your control. What is fully on-chain today: the mint, the revoked authorities, the pool, and your LP tokens. This note will be updated when the wiring lands.

Portfolio

Portfolio is a read-only window onto the chain. Your SOL balance and token holdings are read live from Solana mainnet RPC — nothing is cached or tracked by Vector Pad, and the page also lists the coins you launched in this session.

Look up any wallet

Paste any Solana wallet address into the lookup to see its balances and holdings, read the same way from RPC. Lookups are read-only: looking at a wallet gives you no control over it, and the owner is never notified — addresses and balances are public on Solana by design.

Your stake panel

The Your stake panel shows your stake accounts and their status (activating, active, deactivating, inactive) from the same RPC reads. Deposit takes you to the Stake page to add stake; Redeem takes you to the unstake flow to deactivate and, after cooldown, withdraw. See for how the lifecycle works.

Safety & risks

Verify every address

Before you send funds or sign anything, check the address in full — first characters, last characters, and the middle. Copy addresses from inside this app or your own wallet, never from screenshots, replies or direct messages. A transfer to a wrong or wrong-network address cannot be undone by anyone.

$VECTOR impostor warning

$VECTOR has not launched and has no official contract address. Any token claiming to be $VECTOR — on a DEX, in a group chat, anywhere — is an impostor. The real contract address will be published in this app at launch, and only there. If someone sends you a "$VECTOR" address today, it is fake.

Your seed phrase is yours alone

Never share your seed phrase or private key — with anyone, anywhere. Vector Pad never asks for them: every signature happens inside your own wallet. Any site, form, support agent or "verification" step that asks for your seed phrase is a scam, full stop.

Confirmed means final

Once a transaction is confirmed on-chain it cannot be reversed, edited or refunded — not by Vector Pad, not by the protocol, not by anyone. That is why every flow here simulates first and shows you a summary before your wallet asks you to sign. Read both.

Smart-contract risk

Vector Pad routes through third-party programs — Raydium, Jupiter, Wormhole and others. Bugs, exploits, admin actions or outages in those protocols can cause loss, and new pools and tokens carry more risk than established ones. Bridged assets additionally carry the risk of the bridge itself, and staked SOL is locked by protocol rules during cooldown.

Memecoin risk

Most newly launched tokens lose most or all of their value, quickly and permanently. Never spend funds you cannot afford to lose entirely, and assume any token — including one you create — may become illiquid or worthless. Nothing in this app or these docs is financial advice.

FAQ

Do I need an account to use Vector Pad?

No. There is no sign-up and no account — connect a Solana wallet (Phantom or Solflare) when you want to transact, and browse freely without one.

Does Vector Pad hold my funds?

Never. Vector Pad is a non-custodial front-end: your assets stay in your wallet or in the protocol you are using (a Raydium pool, a stake account, the Wormhole bridge), and every transaction is signed by you.

Is $VECTOR live? What is its contract address?

Not yet — $VECTOR is coming soon and has no contract address. Anything trading under that name today is an impostor. The real address will be published in this app at launch.

What does it cost to launch a coin?

Two modes. Bonding curve: ≈0.01 SOL of rent at create and nothing else — no seed, no pool fee; the pool at graduation is paid from the curve's collected SOL (the program is deployed on mainnet; on a network where it is not deployed, the app says so instead of sending anything). Direct Raydium: ≈0.0035 SOL for the mint plus ≈0.19 SOL to open the Raydium pool, on top of the liquidity you seed and normal network fees. See for the full table.

Why did my transaction stop before signing?

Simulation failed, so nothing was broadcast — that is the safety pattern working. The usual causes are an amount above your balance, a pool that doesn't exist yet, or an invalid validator address. Check the amount and the address first.

Can a transaction be undone or refunded?

No. Once a transaction is confirmed on-chain it is final. Always check the summary line and your wallet's confirmation screen before signing.

Does Vector Pad take a cut of swaps or launches?

No platform fee anywhere. Pools charge Raydium's standard 0.25% trading fee under Raydium's rules, and one-off creation costs are network rent and Raydium's pool cost — Vector Pad receives none of it.
